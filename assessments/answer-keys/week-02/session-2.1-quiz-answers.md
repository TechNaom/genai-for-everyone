# Answer Key — Session 2.1 Quiz

**1.** B — Clarity, context, constraints, and format are the four pillars covered in this chapter.

**2.** "Discuss" is a vague verb describing an activity, with no defined deliverable, length, or structure — the model has to guess what form the answer should take. "Summarize in 3 bullet points" is a specific verb with an explicit format and length, leaving little to guess at.

**3.** C — Missing constraints (length/format) typically don't cause a refusal or a clarifying question; they cause the model to guess at a reasonable length/shape, which may not match what's actually needed downstream.

**4.** Could a competent person, given ONLY this prompt, produce something close to what's actually needed, without having to guess at anything important? If yes, the prompt has enough detail; if you can identify something you knew but never told the model, it needs more.

**5.** Likely missing: context (who is the audience, what's the product, what should be emphasized) and possibly constraints (length, tone) and format. A reasonable one-sentence context addition: "This is for our noise-canceling headphones targeting frequent travelers, emphasizing battery life and comfort on long flights." (Any context addition that supplies a real missing detail should receive credit.)

**6.** Even a long, detailed-looking prompt can be missing ONE specific, critical piece of context the writer assumed was obvious or forgot to include — length and apparent thoroughness don't guarantee that the actually-necessary information is present. This is the Part 4 lesson: the test isn't "is this prompt long," it's "does this prompt contain everything a reader would need to avoid guessing."
