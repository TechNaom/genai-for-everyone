# Quiz — Session 2.1: Anatomy of a Great Prompt

_6 questions, mixed format. Answer key in `assessments/answer-keys/`._

**1. (Multiple choice)** What are the four pillars of a great prompt?
A) Length, tone, grammar, punctuation
B) Clarity, context, constraints, format
C) Speed, cost, capability, compliance
D) Subject, verb, object, modifier

**2. (Short answer)** Why is "discuss the quarterly results" a weaker instruction than "summarize the quarterly results in 3 bullet points"?

**3. (Multiple choice)** A prompt gives a clear task and good context, but no length or format guidance. What's the most likely outcome?
A) The model will refuse to answer
B) The model will ask a clarifying question every time
C) The model will produce a technically valid but possibly unusable response (wrong length or shape)
D) The model will produce the exact same output every time

**4. (Short answer)** What's the practical test for whether a prompt has "enough" detail?

**5. (Scenario)** You ask a model to "write a product description" and get back a generic, unusable paragraph. Diagnose which pillar(s) were likely missing, and write one sentence of context you'd add.

**6. (Scenario)** A long, detailed prompt still produces a wrong-for-purpose answer. Based on this chapter, what's a likely explanation, separate from "the prompt wasn't detailed enough"?
