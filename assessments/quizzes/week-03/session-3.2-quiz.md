# Session 3.2 Quiz — Embeddings Demystified

*6 questions. Mixed multiple-choice and short-answer.*

---

**1.** What is an embedding, in the simplest accurate terms?

A) A compressed version of a text file for storage
B) A list of numbers (a vector) that represents a piece of text's meaning, such that texts with similar meaning produce similar vectors
C) A list of every unique word in a document
D) A summary of a document generated by an LLM

---

**2.** Why can a semantic search system match a query like "how do I get reimbursed for travel" to a document titled "Expense Submission Procedures," even though the two share almost no literal words?

A) The system secretly uses keyword synonyms lists maintained by humans
B) Embeddings compare meaning, not literal vocabulary, so two phrasings of a closely related concept can produce nearby vectors despite little word overlap
C) This isn't actually possible with real embedding-based search
D) The document title is hidden metadata that gets matched separately

---

**3.** Cosine similarity between two vectors primarily measures:

A) How many words the two underlying texts have in common
B) The literal distance between the two vectors' endpoints in space
C) How similar the *direction* the two vectors point in is, ignoring their length/magnitude
D) Whether the two vectors have the same number of dimensions

---

**4. Short answer.** Two vectors have a cosine similarity of approximately 0.95, and two other vectors have a cosine similarity of approximately 0.05. In your own words, what does each of these numbers suggest about the relationship between the underlying pieces of text?

---

**5.** A 2D PCA plot of real sentence embeddings is described in the session as being like "flattening a globe into a 2D map." What is this analogy specifically illustrating?

A) That embeddings are always exactly 2-dimensional internally
B) That reducing high-dimensional vectors down to 2 dimensions for plotting introduces some unavoidable distortion, even though it still preserves a useful approximation of relative distances
C) That maps and embeddings use the same mathematical formula
D) That embedding models were originally designed for geography applications
