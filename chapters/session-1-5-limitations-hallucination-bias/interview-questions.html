Session 1.5 Interview Prep 8 min read

Interview Questions: Limitations, Hallucination & Bias

Every serious interview for a GenAI role assumes you understand this material cold. These two questions come up constantly — interviewers are listening for calm, mechanism-based reasoning and concrete mitigations, not memorized definitions.

9 How would you explain "hallucination" to a product manager who is panicking after seeing a wrong answer from your company's new AI feature?
Strong answer

Stay calm and non-technical: the model wasn't being deceptive, it generated the most statistically plausible-sounding answer from general patterns rather than a verified fact about our specific product — because it isn't connected to our actual documentation. Be honest that no current LLM eliminates this risk entirely. Then point forward to real mitigations: grounding answers in retrieved, verifiable documents (RAG), plus evaluation and guardrails before high-stakes claims.

Red flags

Promising it will "never happen again" — that promise can't be honestly made about any current GenAI system. Also weak: hand-waving that "the next model version will fix it," which misunderstands hallucination as a temporary bug rather than a structural property of how the model generates text.

Follow-up

"So is this just a bug that a better model will patch?" (Answer: no — newer models reduce the frequency, not the fundamental possibility; the structural fix is grounding the output in real data, not waiting for a smarter model.)

10 Where can bias enter a GenAI system, and is it only a "training data" problem?
Strong answer

No — bias is not a single point of failure. It can enter at least four distinct points: training data (over/under-represented perspectives), prompt phrasing (how a question is framed shifts the output), few-shot example selection (the examples you choose to guide the model can encode skew), and evaluation (how "good output" is judged carries its own assumptions). Addressing it requires attention across the whole pipeline.

Red flags

Answering only "biased training data, full stop." If your organization's response to "is our AI biased?" is solely "we checked the training data," you've addressed exactly one of at least four entry points.

Follow-up

"Give me an example of prompt-level bias." (Answer: "Why do many people distrust [group]" presupposes something different than "what are perspectives on [group]" — the answer reflects the framing baked into the question, even with identical underlying facts.)
