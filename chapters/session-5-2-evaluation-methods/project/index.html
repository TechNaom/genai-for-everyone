Session 5.2 Project Pro path 30–40 min build

Routing Logic Stress Test

The Pro-path build for this session — prove, with actual numbers, that the chapter's multi-signal routing rule catches omission failures a naive "flag anything below 0.5 similarity" threshold misses. Like the core exercise, starter.py is fully working already; the point here isn't filling in gaps, it's designing and running the comparison that makes the chapter's central claim concrete and then pushing on it to find where it breaks.

Free and fully offline No API key, no network calls, no paid dependency — this project is pure logic over a hand-built 8-case test set. Every case has a known ground truth (actually_omits_detail) that a human would have determined at golden-dataset build time (Session 5.1), so you can measure each routing strategy's real accuracy on the failure that matters most, not just its overall-looking pass rate.
starter.py solution.py (reference)

The setup

TEST_CASES has 8 clause/summary pairs spanning three clause types, with a known similarity score, LLM-judge score, and ground-truth omission flag for each. Five of the eight genuinely omit a legally significant detail — three of those five (case_2, case_4, case_7) land in the deceptive 0.70–0.85 similarity band the lesson describes, not at the bottom of the scale where a naive threshold would catch them.

Trace it, then run the comparison

1 Trace proper_routing_review() against case_2

case_2 is arbitration, similarity 0.75, LLM-score 5, and genuinely omits a detail. Work out by hand which of the three routing conditions fire for it — is it just one, or more than one? Then check naive_threshold_review(0.75) for the same case. Does the naive rule flag it?

2 Run the full 8-case comparison

Run python starter.py. It runs both naive_threshold_review() and proper_routing_review() against all 8 cases and reports each strategy's false-negative rate specifically among the 5 real omission cases, plus its overall flag rate. Confirm the printed false-negative rate for the naive strategy matches what you'd expect given how many of the 5 omission cases sit above 0.5 similarity (3 of them: case_2, case_4, case_7).

3 Find where the "proper" rule still isn't perfect

Check the per-case output for proper_routing_review() carefully — does it actually catch all 5 omission cases, or does one still slip through? Look specifically at case_3: notice_period (not a high-risk type), LLM-score 3 (not in the 4–7 uncertain band), similarity 0.40 (not in the 0.70–0.85 band, and not even close). It's an "obviously bad" case a human would catch instantly — and the naive threshold does catch it — but the proper rule's three conditions were built around the deceptive-middle-band problem specifically, not around catching every low score too. Explain in a comment why a routing rule built entirely from these three signals can still miss a genuinely bad case that happens to score low on everything, and what you'd add to close that specific gap without just reintroducing the naive rule's whole class of misses.

VERDICT
Naive threshold:  60% of real omissions missed, 25% overall flag rate
Proper routing:   20% of real omissions missed, 62% overall flag rate
Why this matters A lower false-negative rate on the failures that matter most is worth a higher overall review workload — that trade-off is the point of human-in-the-loop routing, not a flaw in it. Measuring "false negatives among the cases that actually matter" instead of overall accuracy is what makes that trade-off visible and defensible, rather than hidden inside a single aggregate number that treats every case as equally important.

Why this project matters

An eval harness that only reports "% correct overall" can look excellent while still missing most of the failures a legal or medical reviewer would consider unacceptable, simply because those failures are a minority of the total cases. Measuring false negatives specifically among the failure type you care about most — and being honest about where even a well-designed routing rule still has a gap — is the same discipline a real evaluation team applies before shipping anything to production.
